How does it work?

Every device on your network — your phone, your laptop, your smart TV — needs to look up website addresses before it can connect to them. That lookup is called a DNS request. DNS works like a phone book — you look up a name and get back a number. That number tells your browser where to go on the Internet.

HoneytrapAI sits in the middle of that process. When a device asks "where is badsite.com?", HoneytrapAI checks it against known threat lists and blocks it if it's dangerous — before any connection is made.

For this to work, HoneytrapAI needs to be your network's DNS server. The setup wizard will walk you through that. It also means that if a device is using its own private DNS (sometimes called DNS-over-HTTPS or DoH), HoneytrapAI won't be able to see or protect that device's traffic.

Before you go
A couple of things to know before you start using your dashboard.
📡 Point your router to HoneytrapAI

Your router needs to use HoneytrapAI as its primary DNS server. Set the primary DNS to {{ device_ip or 'your HoneytrapAI IP address' }}. Good secondary DNS choices are Cloudflare at 1.1.1.1 or Google at 8.8.8.8. Not sure how? Web search "change DNS server" plus your router's brand name, or check our help section in the dashboard.

🔒 Avoid private DNS on your devices

Some phones and browsers have a setting called Private DNS, Secure DNS, or DNS-over-HTTPS. If enabled, it bypasses HoneytrapAI. Turn it off on any browser you want covered — with HoneytrapAI working, you'll actually be safer!
